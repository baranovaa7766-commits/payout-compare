Wise vs Revolut vs Bank vs Crypto for Prop Traders

Once your prop firm approves a payout, converting it to your local currency is a separate decision — and it can cost you several percent either way. Here's how the four common methods actually compare.

Wise

Wise converts at the mid-market rate and adds a small, published markup — usually the most transparent option and often the best value for a straight USD/EUR/GBP-to-local-currency conversion. Transfers typically land in 1-2 days. There's no large hidden spread to watch for, which makes it easy to sanity-check against the calculator on the homepage before you send.

Revolut

Revolut can be just as competitive as Wise on weekdays within your plan's free FX allowance, and it's often faster. The catch: fair-usage limits and weekend/out-of-hours fees can quietly add a percent or more if you're not on a paid plan or you convert outside market hours. Check your plan's terms before assuming the free rate applies.

Bank transfer (SWIFT)

The default option if you do nothing — your bank receives the payout and converts it automatically. Convenient, but banks typically mark up the exchange rate well below mid-market (often 2-4%) and add a flat wire fee on top ($15-45 is common). For larger payouts this is usually the most expensive method in absolute terms, even though it requires no extra setup.

Crypto (USDT and similar)

If your prop firm pays out in crypto, or you choose to route funds through USDT, the trade-off shifts: settlement can be near-instant, but you're now exposed to network fees and — critically — the spread charged by whichever exchange or on/off-ramp you use to convert USDT back into your local currency. That final cash-out leg is where most of the real cost hides.

So which is best?

For most traders converting USD or EUR payouts into a major local currency, Wise or Revolut (within free-plan limits) will out-perform a bank transfer, often by 2-4% of the payout. Crypto is worth it mainly when speed matters more than a percent or two, or when it's the only method your prop firm and country support well. There's no universal answer — run your actual payout amount and currencies through the calculator on the homepage, since fees and rates shift with amount, currency pair, and provider terms.
